A learning app can extend your teaching beyond the classroom, give students more practice and show you where they struggle. It can also become an expensive project that nobody opens after the first week. The difference is usually made before any code is written. This checklist covers the decisions we work through with every school, coaching institute and publisher before we start building.

1. Who are the learners, and what should they be able to do?

Be specific. “Class 10 students preparing for board exams in science” leads to a very different app from “working adults learning cloud skills”. Write down:

  • the age group and level of the learners;
  • the devices and internet access they have;
  • two or three outcomes, such as “solve previous-year questions within the time limit”.

Every feature you consider later should help with one of these outcomes.

2. Where will the content come from?

Content is usually the largest piece of work. Decide early whether you will use your existing notes, question banks and videos, or create new material. Check that you own the rights to everything you plan to include. Also decide who will update content after launch, and how often.

3. Does it need to work offline?

For many learners in India, data is limited or the connection is unreliable. An app that downloads lessons and quizzes for offline use will be used far more than one that needs a constant connection. Offline support affects the design from the start, so decide on it before development begins.

4. Which platforms?

Android reaches the large majority of students in India, so most projects start there. Add iOS if a significant share of your learners or their parents use iPhones, and a web version if teachers or administrators need to work on a computer. Building a single design system for all of them keeps the experience consistent.

5. How will learners practise and get feedback?

Practice is where learning happens. Consider:

  • question types: multiple choice, numerical answers, matching, diagrams;
  • instant explanations for wrong answers;
  • timed mock tests that mirror the real exam;
  • progress tracking, streaks and revision reminders that bring learners back.

6. What do teachers and administrators need?

An admin panel to add content, manage batches, see results and send announcements is often as important as the student app. List these needs early, because they shape the back end.

7. Privacy, children and store policies

If you collect personal data, you need a clear privacy policy and a plan for storing data securely. In India, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 sets out obligations for handling personal data, including special care for children’s data. If your app is aimed at children, Google Play’s Families policies also apply, including rules on ads and data collection. Collect only what you need.

8. Budget, timeline and phases

Launch a focused first version, then improve it based on how learners actually use it. A typical path is:

  1. discovery and a written plan;
  2. a clickable prototype to test with a few students;
  3. a first release with the core learning features;
  4. updates that add content and features based on data.

Set aside budget for maintenance. Operating system updates, store policy changes and new content all need ongoing work.

9. Who owns what?

Agree in writing who owns the source code, the content and the store listing, and what happens if you change developers later. Ask for the app to be published under your own Google Play and App Store accounts if you want full control.

Quick checklist

  • Learners, devices and outcomes written down
  • Content source, rights and update plan agreed
  • Offline needs decided
  • Platforms chosen
  • Practice, feedback and progress features listed
  • Admin needs listed
  • Privacy policy and store policies reviewed
  • Phased budget and timeline, including maintenance
  • Ownership agreed in writing
