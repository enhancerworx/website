How to memorise world flags and capitals: 7 techniques that work

Learn the world's flags and capitals faster with seven proven techniques: group by region, spot flag families, learn the tricky capitals and test yourself.

By Enhancerworx team
24 November 2026 · 3 min read

Study tips

There are close to two hundred countries and dozens of territories, each with a flag and a capital. Learned as one long list, that is overwhelming. Learned with a bit of structure, it becomes a satisfying puzzle. These seven techniques are the ones we see work best for learners using our Learn World Flags and Capitals app.

1. Learn by region, not alphabetically

Alphabetical lists put Chad next to Chile and Chile next to China, which tells your brain nothing. Learn one continent or region at a time instead. Neighbouring countries often share history, colours and naming patterns, so they reinforce each other. Start with the region you know best, then move outward.

2. Spot the flag families

Many flags belong to families with a shared design. Once you know the family, you only need to learn how each member differs.

  • Nordic crosses: Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland and Iceland all use an off-centre cross.
  • Pan-African colours: red, gold and green, inspired by Ethiopia’s flag, appear on the flags of Ghana, Senegal, Mali, Guinea, Cameroon and others.
  • Pan-Arab colours: red, white, green and black appear on the flags of Jordan, the United Arab Emirates, Kuwait, Sudan and Palestine.
  • Southern Cross: Australia, New Zealand, Papua New Guinea and Samoa show this constellation.

3. Master the look-alikes

A handful of flags are almost identical, and quizzes love them. Learn them as pairs:

  • Chad and Romania: both are blue, yellow and red vertical stripes; Chad’s blue is darker.
  • Indonesia and Monaco: both are red over white; Monaco’s flag is less wide for its height.
  • Ireland and Côte d’Ivoire: the same three colours in opposite order. Ireland starts with green at the flagpole; Côte d’Ivoire starts with orange.

4. Learn the capitals that break the rule

Most people assume a country’s largest or best-known city is its capital. Often it isn’t. These are worth learning on purpose:

  • Australia: Canberra, not Sydney
  • Canada: Ottawa, not Toronto
  • Turkey: Ankara, not Istanbul
  • Brazil: Brasília, not Rio de Janeiro
  • Nigeria: Abuja, not Lagos
  • Myanmar: Naypyidaw, not Yangon
  • Kazakhstan: Astana, not Almaty

Some countries have more than one capital. South Africa divides its functions between Pretoria, Cape Town and Bloemfontein, and Bolivia’s constitutional capital is Sucre while its government sits in La Paz.

5. Attach a story or an image

Facts with meaning stick. Find out why a flag looks the way it does: the maple leaf of Canada, the cedar tree of Lebanon or the Ashoka Chakra on India’s flag. For capitals, make a quick mental image that links the two names. The sillier the image, the better it tends to work.

6. Test yourself in both directions

Knowing that Ottawa is Canada’s capital does not mean you will recognise Ottawa in a list. Practise both directions: country to capital, and capital to country. Do the same for flags: see the flag and name the country, then see the country and pick the flag.

Tip: Games are an easy way to get this practice. The flip-and-match and drag-and-drop games in our flags app test both directions, and you can limit quizzes to one continent while you learn it.

7. Little and often

Five or ten minutes a day is enough. Revise yesterday’s region quickly, then add a few new countries. Return to your mistakes at the end of each session. Over a few weeks you will cover the whole world without ever having a long, tiring study session.

Put the flags on a map

Finally, connect what you learn to a map. Knowing where a country sits helps you remember its neighbours, its region and often its flag family. Our Learn World Map app lets you tap countries on a map, which pairs well with flag practice.
